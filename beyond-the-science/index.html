Beyond the Science

Teaching, mentorship, and life beyond the lab.

Teaching and Mentorship

My goal as a teacher and mentor is to help people develop the understanding, confidence, and judgment to think independently, take ownership of their work, and pursue paths that reflect their interests and strengths.

Teaching for understanding

I want students to be able to explain their reasoning and apply what they learn to unfamiliar problems. I emphasize connections between mathematical models, physical intuition, and practical engineering questions. Short problems, discussion, and written responses give students different ways to participate, test their understanding, and learn from one another. Clear expectations and constructive feedback help make challenging material approachable for students with different preparation.

Building independence through support

I believe high expectations work best alongside strong support. Guidance should evolve as each person gains experience. Regular conversations, clear goals, and thoughtful feedback provide a foundation for trainees to take increasing responsibility for their work. My aim is to help them develop their own questions, evaluate evidence, and decide which directions are worth pursuing.

Creating a culture of openness and responsibility

I want to build a research environment where people feel comfortable asking questions, discussing setbacks, and challenging interpretations. This openness comes with responsibility for data integrity, reproducibility, and fair credit. Transparent expectations about project roles, authorship, and professional opportunities help people work together with trust. I value both ownership of individual projects and the willingness to share methods, ideas, and expertise.

Supporting individual paths

Career development is an essential part of mentorship. I value conversations about personal goals alongside discussions of scientific progress. Writing, presenting, collaborating, and teaching provide opportunities to build skills that extend beyond any single project. I want trainees to leave with the confidence and preparation to pursue careers that suit them, whether in academia, industry, or another setting.

Family and Friendship

Many of the friendships I value most began through working and learning together. People I first knew as mentors, students, collaborators, or colleagues have become friends whose place in my life extends well beyond research. We’ve shared the excitement of making progress and the frustration of setbacks, and we continue to learn from, encourage, and support one another. At the center of my life is my family. I’m grateful for my wife’s support and for the company of our two cats, who bring warmth and joy to everyday life. My family makes home a place where I can recharge, be myself, and find the encouragement to keep going.
