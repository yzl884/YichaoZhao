Understanding and controlling biology at the molecular level

Yichao Zhao

Dr. Yichao Zhao is currently a postdoctoral research fellow co-advised by Professors Daniel Anderson and Robert Langer at MIT. He earned his Ph.D. in Materials Science and Engineering under the supervision of Professor Sam Emaminejad at UCLA in 2021.

His research integrates materials design, chemical synthesis, and synthetic biology to develop new bioelectronic technologies for multiscale biomarker sensing and precise control of cellular functions.

Research Overview

Bioelectronics such as smartwatches, neural stimulators, and pacemakers have transformed our ability to monitor and modulate physical signals. The next frontier is extending these technologies to continuously monitor biochemical markers and deliver therapeutics, enabling a more decentralized and personalized model of healthcare.

I develop tissue-like bioelectronics for continuous in vivo biochemical monitoring. To overcome signal distortion caused by tissue deformation, I developed strain-insensitive electrochemical materials that decouple mechanical interference from true biochemical readouts. These tools enable high-fidelity pharmacokinetic studies in interstitial and cerebrospinal fluids.

I also develop cell-based implants as “living medicines.” While promising, their translation is hindered by a lack of precise control over therapeutic secretion in vivo. My work introduces a new class of solid-state electrolytes that bioelectronically modulate endogenous ion pathways in mammalian cells, enabling the rapid, on-demand release of therapeutic proteins.
